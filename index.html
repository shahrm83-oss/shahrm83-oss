Materials Science
MSE 306 Research · 2026

Corrosion in Coastal PV Mounting Systems

// Overview

Photovoltaic mounting systems along the northeastern US coastline face accelerated degradation from salt-laden air, moisture cycling, and temperature fluctuations. This study investigates the corrosion performance of AA6063-T6 aluminium and hot-dip galvanised steel — two materials widely used in PV racking — and evaluates protective treatments to extend service life beyond the typical 25–30 year design target.

// What I Did

Analysed corrosion mechanisms affecting AA6063-T6 in marine environments, focusing on intergranular corrosion and pitting. Evaluated anodisation as a surface treatment and assessed hot-dip galvanising on steel hardware. Reviewed electrochemical data and corrosion rate models relevant to Boston's climate and produced material selection and protective coating recommendations.

// Key Findings
  • AA6063-T6 with a 25 µm hard-anodised coating significantly outperforms untreated aluminium in chloride-rich environments
  • Galvanic coupling between aluminium frames and steel fasteners is a primary failure mode — isolation washers recommended
  • Hot-dip galvanised steel requires additional barrier coatings in direct coastal exposure zones
  • Anodised aluminium identified as the preferred primary structural material for Boston-area coastal PV racking
